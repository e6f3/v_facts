How to check a fact in 5 steps

The most useful thing OpenFacts can teach you. Use it on anything you read.

  1. Who is saying it?Is the source an expert, a primary source, or a re-post of a re-post? Trace the claim back to where it started.
  2. What's the evidence?Look for data, experiments, records or direct observations, not just confident language.
  3. Do others agree?Check whether independent, reliable sources reach the same conclusion. One surprising study is a clue, not a verdict.
  4. Is it current?Facts about the world change: populations, records, prices and even scientific estimates get updated.
  5. How big, compared to what?Numbers need context. "Doubles the risk" of something very rare may still be tiny.

Milestones of Human Knowledge

Facts are discovered by people. Here are a few big moments.

  • 1543 Copernicus publishes a model with the Sun at the center of the planets' orbits.
  • 1687 Isaac Newton publishes the Principia, laying out laws of motion and gravity.
  • 1859 Charles Darwin publishes On the Origin of Species, explaining evolution by natural selection.
  • 1953 Watson and Crick, building on work by Rosalind Franklin and others, describe the double-helix structure of DNA.
  • 1969 Apollo 11 lands the first humans on the Moon.
  • 1991 The first website goes live at CERN, launching the World Wide Web for public use.
  • 2012 CERN scientists announce the discovery of the Higgs boson.
  • 2015 LIGO detects gravitational waves for the first time (announced in 2016).

A note from OpenFacts: I simplify to make ideas easy to grasp. For anything important, check reputable sources such as scientific journals, museums, universities, and national agencies. Good thinkers update their beliefs when better evidence arrives.
